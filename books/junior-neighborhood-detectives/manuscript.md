## Page 3: Welcome to the club

A clue is something you can check. A guess is an idea you have not checked yet.

Good detectives use both. They make a guess, look at the clues, and change their minds when they need to.

Maya likes to look closely. Sam likes to try an idea. Jo asks, “Could another answer fit?” You can do all three.

Our club rule: solve the mix-up, not blame a person.


## Page 4: Your detective toolkit

Use a pencil and an eraser. Write, circle, or draw an answer. Ask a grown-up to read with you if you like.

Each case has four pages: read the story, investigate, check, and explain. Take a break whenever you want.

Hints begin on page 89. Explained answers begin on page 99. Help is not a penalty.

You can skip a case. The final chapter repeats every fact it needs.


## Page 5: Keep investigations safe

Use the printed records. Do not taste unknown liquids, touch unknown plants, handle small magnets, or test electricity or heat.

Never follow a clue into an unfamiliar place. Stay with a trusted grown-up outdoors.

Some records describe tests done by an adult. You are reading those results, not being asked to do the experiment.

A wrong guess is not a bad person. Check the evidence and try again.


## Page 6: Words that help

Observe: notice something carefully. Record: save what was observed.

Evidence: information that helps answer a question. Compare: look for differences or similarities.

Eliminate: rule out a choice using a clue. Explain: say why the evidence fits.

A fair comparison changes one thing while keeping the other listed conditions alike.


## Page 7: Try one together

Two envelopes are marked A and B. One holds a circle card; the other holds a square card. The note says A holds the square.

Question: Where is the circle?

Work it out: A is taken by the square. Each envelope holds just one card. B is the only place left.

Answer: the circle is in B. Notice that we did not guess from envelope size or color. We used the note and the one-card rule.
